The part homeowners cannot see

The clay sheds water. The underlayment keeps it out.

A beautiful clay roof can still leak when the hidden waterproofing and metal details have aged or were damaged.

1

Clay tile is the first layer

Tile slows and redirects most rain, but it is not the final waterproof barrier. Wind-driven water can reach the underlayment below.

2

Flashing controls the hard areas

Chimneys, walls, valleys, pipes and solar attachments interrupt the field of tile. Those transition details must move water back onto the roof.

3

The hidden materials age first

The clay may remain reusable while felt, fasteners, mortar and older flashing become brittle, corroded, clogged or separated.

Replacing one broken clay tile does not automatically repair a leak.

The correct repair follows the drainage path, opens enough tile to inspect below and ties new waterproofing into sound material.

What we find in the field

Common causes of clay tile roof leaks

01

Broken, slipped or glued clay tile

Clay is fragile. Broken pieces and failed adhesive repairs expose the underlayment and let debris enter the water channel.

02

Aged underlayment

Old felt dries out, tears around fasteners and loses the flexibility needed to drain water beneath the tile.

03

Outdated or failed flashing

Wall, chimney, pipe and valley metal may be undersized, corroded or buried under mortar. Sometimes the detail must be rebuilt—not caulked.

04

Solar installation damage

Foot traffic can crack two-piece clay, disturb courses and leave underlayment exposed beside panels or attachment points.

05

Debris and neglected maintenance

Leaves, mortar fragments and broken clay collect in pans and valleys, redirecting water sideways until it wears the paper below.

06

Failed mortar at hips and walls

Mortar cracks with age and movement. Surface patches may hide the opening temporarily without restoring the drainage detail.

Solar and clay tile

Solar work can leave damage hidden beside and beneath the panels

Two-piece clay is easily broken by foot traffic. We regularly find displaced pieces, broken pan tile, exposed battens or underlayment and mortar used where a proper tile or flashing detail was needed.

Before solar is installed—or when panels are removed for roofing—the clay work should be planned by someone who understands how the roof drains.

A panel can hide the damage from view, but it does not protect exposed underlayment from aging and water movement.

30+years deserves a broader underlayment assessment

The clay may still be good when the roof beneath it is worn out

Clay tile itself can remain usable far longer than the original felt or flashing. That is why an older clay roof may look attractive from the street while developing leaks around walls, chimneys, solar, valleys and penetrations.

A limited repair can still make sense when damage is truly isolated. When the paper is brittle across multiple openings or leaks keep appearing in new locations, repeatedly patching small areas may cost more than addressing a full slope or completing a lift-and-lay.

Specialized workmanship matters

Two-piece clay is slower, more fragile and more expensive to handle

Each course uses separate pan and cover pieces. Walking, lifting, stacking and reinstalling them without unnecessary breakage takes more time than working with most concrete tile.

Matching can also be difficult when clay is discontinued or weathered. Zenith has worked on these roofs and understands how to preserve reusable material, distribute compatible replacements and rebuild the waterproofing without turning a valuable roof into a patchwork.

The repair price reflects the labor and care required to protect expensive clay—not simply the square footage opened.

Clay roof repair FAQ

Questions homeowners ask before opening a clay roof

Is two-piece clay more expensive to repair than concrete tile?

Usually. The material is fragile and expensive, and safe removal, staging, matching and reinstallation take more labor. The roof also needs workers experienced with separate pan and cover pieces.

Can a broken clay tile simply be glued?

Adhesive may be used as a short-term measure in limited circumstances, but it is not equivalent to an intact tile. Heat, movement and weather can cause glued pieces to fail. The better repair is replacement plus inspection of the paper below.

Can the original clay tile be reused during a lift-and-lay?

Yes. Serviceable clay is carefully removed, staged and reinstalled. A breakage allowance and compatible replacement material are still needed because old clay can break during handling.

How long does clay roof underlayment last?

There is no single guaranteed age. Original material, heat, drainage, maintenance and installation all matter. Many older roofs need a closer underlayment assessment around the 25- to 30-year range even when the clay remains reusable.
