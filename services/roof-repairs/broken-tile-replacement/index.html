Do not ignore a broken tile

One broken tile can leave the roof below it vulnerable.

A roof tile is part of a layered system. When it breaks, slips, or is missing, the underlayment and flashing below can be more exposed to water, debris, and UV. A broken tile does not automatically mean there is a leak, but it is worth addressing before a small opening becomes a larger repair.

The practical goal

Restore a complete, protected roof surface.

We assess the broken unit, surrounding tiles, and accessible roof layer below. If the issue is isolated and the area beneath is sound, an individual replacement may be the right solution. If we find an underlying problem, we explain it with photos and recommend only the next appropriate repair.

How Zenith approaches it

A proper individual tile replacement has real steps behind it.

That is not padding the scope. It is what helps ensure the replacement belongs in the roof system and the repair is not simply a visible cover-up.

  1. 01

    Identify the profile

    We examine the tile shape, size, interlock, and color family. Concrete tile profiles are not interchangeable.

  2. 02

    Source the closest practical match

    We check suppliers and, when necessary, salvage sources for discontinued products. Availability determines whether an exact match is possible.

  3. 03

    Open and inspect carefully

    We remove damaged pieces with tile-roof access in mind and inspect the accessible area below for damage that should be addressed.

  4. 04

    Install and finish correctly

    We install the full replacement tile with the appropriate compatible securement detail, protect any applicable exposed penetration, clean up debris, and dispose of broken material.

A full tile is better than rejoining fragments

Why we do not recommend gluing two broken pieces back together as the final repair.

Adhesive can be part of an approved securement detail, but using it to rejoin a broken tile is not the same as replacing the tile. The break can remain a weak point, adhesives can weather and lose adhesion, and a glued surface repair does not confirm that the layer beneath the tile is sound.

Tile roofs near solar equipment deserve particular attention because work and foot traffic can occur around the roof covering. We focus on the roof repair itself: the complete tile, the correct profile, and the condition of the area underneath.

Our standard: Replace the broken unit when a suitable tile can be sourced—then inspect and complete the repair detail rather than relying on a cosmetic glue line.

The part homeowners do not see

Tile matching is a material-sourcing job.

We want the replacement to fit and look right, but we do not promise a perfect visual match where the manufacturer, profile, or color has been discontinued. Existing tile also changes color over time. Our promise is a careful profile match and the closest practical available color—without forcing you to buy an unnecessary pallet or a collection of samples.

Profile matters

Flat, low-profile, S, and barrel tiles have different dimensions and interlocks. A tile that looks close may not seat or shed water correctly.

Availability matters

Some tiles remain stocked; others require a specialty supplier or salvage-yard search. We tell you what we can source before proceeding.

Access and logistics matter

Safe roof access, sourcing and pickup, careful removal, installation, cleanup, and disposal are part of an honest individual-tile repair.

Straight answers

Broken concrete tile replacement questions.

Can you replace just one broken concrete roof tile?

Yes, when the damage is isolated and a compatible tile can be sourced. We also inspect the accessible area beneath it so the repair addresses more than the visible tile.

Can you match the color of my existing roof tile?

We work to find the closest practical match. Exact color cannot be guaranteed because tile products may be discontinued and existing tiles weather at different rates.

Can a broken tile simply be glued back together?

We do not recommend treating a glued-together broken tile as a final roof repair. It is not a substitute for an intact replacement tile or inspection of the area below it.

Why is individual tile replacement more involved than it looks?

A professional repair includes profile identification, sourcing, safe access, careful removal, inspection of the roof area beneath, compatible installation, cleanup, and disposal. Those steps help protect the roof system—not just improve its appearance.

What should I do while I wait for a repair?

Avoid walking the roof or trying to glue pieces in place. Take clear photos from a safe location and request an inspection, especially if you see a missing tile, a visible opening, or any interior water staining.
