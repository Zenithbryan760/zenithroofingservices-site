Start with the roof—not the stain

A flat-roof leak is a symptom. The assembly tells us how to repair it.

A ceiling stain can be several feet from the entry point. Water may travel beneath the membrane, through insulation or along deck joints before it shows inside.

01

Same symptom, different repair

An open TPO seam is not repaired like a split in APP modified bitumen or a failed detail buried beneath gravel. We identify the existing system before specifying materials.

02

Trace the water path

We inspect laps, transitions, walls, curbs, pipes, scuppers, drains and the surrounding field—not only the spot directly above the interior damage.

03

Judge the roof around it

A focused repair makes sense when the adjacent roof can support it. Widespread moisture, repeated leaks or a worn-out system can change the honest recommendation.

Systems we service

Repair options for virtually every common flat roof.

Compatibility matters. The new membrane, adhesive, reinforcing and surfacing must work with the roof that remains in place.

Multi-ply

Built-Up Roofing (BUR)

Asphalt and reinforcing plies, often surfaced with gravel. Repairs may require clearing aggregate, opening failed layers, replacing damaged material, reinforcing transitions and restoring the surfacing.

Modified bitumen

APP / SBS & Torch-Down

Localized repairs can use compatible base, smooth and cap materials, installed by an appropriate torch-applied, cold-process or other approved method for the existing assembly.

Low-flame options

Self-Adhered & Cold Process

Multi-ply repairs can be built with self-adhered membranes or cold-process adhesives where those materials and application methods fit the roof and site conditions.

Single-ply

TPO & PVC

Open seams, punctures and failed details may be cleaned, prepared and repaired with compatible membrane and heat-welded patches after moisture and substrate conditions are checked.

Rubber membrane

EPDM

EPDM requires system-compatible cleaners, primers, cover tape, uncured flashing or membrane patches. Generic roof cement is not a substitute for a compatible detail.

Restoration

Silicone-Coated Roofs

Details and damaged coating can often be cleaned, reinforced and recoated. A broader restoration may be considered only when the existing roof is dry, sound and compatible.

Where flat roofs leak

Most leaks begin at a transition, opening or stressed detail.

The roof field matters, but penetrations and drainage details receive movement, water and service traffic that can accelerate failure.

Seams, laps & splits

Open welds, fishmouths, wrinkles, punctures and membrane movement can create a direct path beneath the roof.

Drains & scuppers

Loose clamping rings, failed tie-ins, blocked flow and deteriorated sealant can keep water at the most vulnerable point.

Pipes, curbs & penetrations

HVAC curbs, pipe flashings, pitch pockets, supports and conduits all interrupt the waterproofing plane.

Walls, parapets & edges

Terminations, counterflashing, edge metal and wall transitions move differently than the roof field and need durable tie-ins.

Wet insulation or deck

Trapped moisture and soft substrate can spread beyond the visible opening. Unsound material must be addressed before it is covered.

Traffic & rooftop equipment

Solar work, HVAC service and repeated foot traffic can puncture or stress the roof around busy access paths and equipment zones.

The method follows the system

Several ways to make a durable flat-roof repair.

The right choice comes from field conditions—not from forcing one product onto every roof.

Single-ply

Membrane patch or seam repair

For compatible TPO, PVC or EPDM, the area is cleaned and prepared before a system-specific patch, seam or flashing repair is installed.

Modified bitumen

Base, ply and cap repair

A torch-down opening may be rebuilt with compatible base material, a smooth or intermediate ply and an APP cap sheet, depending on the existing assembly.

Alternative application

Self-adhered or cold process

Self-adhered membranes and cold-process adhesives can build a compatible multi-ply repair where site conditions or the roof system favor those methods.

Detail work

Three-course reinforcement

Roofing cement with embedded fiberglass reinforcing fabric and a protective top layer can strengthen compatible BUR and modified-bitumen transitions and details.

Water management

Drain or scupper rebuild

Localized work may include opening the surrounding roof, correcting the tie-in, resealing or replacing components and restoring positive drainage where feasible.

Restoration option

Silicone detail or coating work

On a suitable dry roof, silicone can simplify restoration around extensive solar, HVAC and electrical equipment—but coating is not a cure for trapped moisture.

Repair, restore or replace?

We explain what the repair can—and cannot—do.

A repair can be the right investment, a practical bridge or poor value. The difference is the condition of the roof around the leak.

Spot or detail repair

Best fit for a localized puncture, seam, pipe, curb, drain or scupper when the nearby roof is dry and serviceable.

Sectional opening

Used when the water path or damaged material extends beyond a small surface detail and a larger area must be rebuilt.

Recovery or restoration

May fit a compatible roof with sound, dry substrate—especially where a crowded equipment layout makes tear-off disruptive.

Replacement

Often the stronger value when wet insulation, widespread deterioration, repeated leaks or end-of-life materials are no longer localized.

An honest note about old BUR roofs

Sometimes a patch is an expensive Band-Aid.

Tar-and-gravel roofs can be repaired, but an isolated repair does not renew the surrounding aged roof. If the system is brittle, saturated or leaking in several areas, we will tell you before you spend money expecting a new-roof result.

  • The proposed repair scope is limited to the areas we work on.
  • Adjacent old roofing can develop a separate leak later.
  • We discuss those limits and the applicable workmanship coverage before work begins.

Flat roof repair FAQ

Every assembly is different, but these are the questions we answer most often before a repair.

What types of flat roofs do you repair?

We repair built-up and tar-and-gravel roofs, APP and SBS modified bitumen, torch-down and self-adhered systems, TPO, PVC, EPDM and compatible silicone-coated roofs. We first identify the existing system so the repair materials and method match it.

Can you repair a tar-and-gravel roof?

Sometimes. A localized repair can be reasonable when the surrounding built-up roof is still serviceable. On a very old, brittle or broadly deteriorated roof, the same repair may only be a temporary and relatively expensive bridge. We will explain that distinction before work begins.

Why can the ceiling stain be far from the roof leak?

Water can travel beneath roofing layers, through insulation, along deck joints or with the roof slope before it appears inside. We inspect likely entry points and transitions rather than assuming the stain marks the exact opening.

Can you repair scuppers, drains, pipes and HVAC curbs?

Yes. These are common leak points. A focused repair may involve opening the surrounding membrane, rebuilding the tie-in, reinforcing or replacing flashing materials, resealing components and restoring drainage.

When does silicone coating make sense?

A compatible silicone restoration may make sense when the existing roof is dry and serviceable, particularly on a busy roof with solar panels, HVAC equipment and extensive electrical conduit. It is not a shortcut over wet insulation or unsound roofing.

Can you repair a flat roof around solar panels and equipment?

Often, yes. Access, penetrations, equipment supports and service paths all affect the scope. We assess whether a spot repair is practical or whether a compatible coating or larger sectional repair provides better continuity.

How long will a flat roof repair last?

There is no responsible one-number answer. Service life depends on the system, moisture, attachment, drainage, age and condition of the surrounding roof, and whether the leak is truly localized. We explain the likely limitations with the proposed scope.
