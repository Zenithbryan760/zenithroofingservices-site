The part most homeowners never see

Tile is the first line of defense—not the waterproofing layer

A repair only makes sense when it addresses how the entire roof assembly moves water.

1

Tile slows and sheds rain

Concrete or clay tile protects the roof from direct weather, but wind-driven rain can still pass through joints, valleys, broken pieces and transitions.

2

Flashing directs concentrated water

Chimneys, skylights, pipes, walls and valleys interrupt the roof slope. Their metal pans, saddles and counterflashing must guide water back onto the roof.

3

Underlayment protects the deck

The paper or synthetic membrane below the tile is the final water-shedding layer. Once it is brittle, torn or worn through, moving a tile does not restore it.

If it leaked once and suddenly stopped, that does not mean it repaired itself.

The next storm may simply be shorter, lighter, or coming from a different direction. A damaged underlayment path usually leaks again when the right conditions return.

Where we look first

Why tile roofs leak in San Diego and Temecula

Age is often the underlying cause, but water usually shows itself at a roof interruption or neglected drainage detail.

01

Chimneys and skylights

Water hits the wall behind the structure and collects at the saddle, pan, headwall and sidewall flashing. Debris can hold moisture and push water sideways for years.

02

Valleys cut too tight

Tile installed too close to the valley metal restricts flow and traps leaves, dirt and moss. A closed valley can force water sideways beneath the tile.

03

Pipes, vents and failed mortar

Cracked sealant, loose mortar, poorly detailed pipe flashing, missing weather blocking or absent edge metal can create direct water-entry paths.

04

Slipped and broken tiles

Replacing or resetting tile helps limit water reaching the underlayment. It does not repair underlayment that is already ripped, punctured or deteriorated.

05

Deferred maintenance

Uncleaned valleys, loose tiles and neglected flashing let small conditions compound. Maintenance can reduce exposure, but it cannot reverse existing underlayment damage.

06

Underlayment age

Tile may remain attractive for decades while the material below ages out. The repair decision must be based on the condition under the tile, not the tile appearance alone.

Why we avoid tiny spot patches

Water does not always travel straight down

Flat tile roofs can allow water to move sideways beneath the tile. The interior stain may also appear downhill from the actual opening. Repairing only a two-foot area around the stain can leave the real weakness untouched.

When conditions call for it, we open a meaningful section—often approximately 10 feet wide—and follow the drainage path from the eave upward to sound tie-in points. Depending on the roof, that may mean carrying the repair over a ridge or hip, or tying it into a rake, sidewall or adjacent facet.

The goal is not to make the smallest patch possible. The goal is to create a continuous, correctly layered path for water to leave the roof.
30Years deserves a broader look

Older tile can look good while the underlayment is at the end of its service life

There is no maintenance product that makes aged-out underlayment new again. Cleaning valleys, replacing broken tiles and maintaining flashing can reduce exposure and prevent avoidable damage, but existing deterioration remains.

If a tile roof is around 30 years old, we do not automatically recommend a patch. We inspect beyond the immediate leak and explain whether a sectional repair, a full-slope partial lift-and-lay, or a broader reset is the better use of your budget.

Straight answers

Tile roof repair FAQs

Does pushing a slipped tile back into place fix the leak?

It may reduce how much rain reaches the underlayment, but it does not repair underlayment that is already torn or deteriorated. If the roof has produced a clear interior leak, the waterproofing below the tile should be evaluated.

Can one broken tile cause a leak?

A broken tile increases direct exposure, but a serviceable underlayment should still shed incidental water. An interior leak may indicate a puncture, failed flashing or aged underlayment below the broken tile.

Why are chimneys and skylights common leak areas?

They block the natural downslope flow of water. Debris can collect behind them, and their saddles, pans, headwall, sidewall and counterflashing contain multiple transitions that must remain open and correctly layered.

Can a roof vent leak only during heavy rain?

Yes. O’Hagin-style vents, dormer vents and other attic or exhaust openings can admit wind-driven rain under certain conditions. We separate those ventilation-entry issues from a failed roof assembly during diagnosis.

Why might you recommend a 10-foot-wide repair?

The exact width is condition-dependent, but water can travel sideways beneath flat tile and along compromised underlayment. A broader opening lets us find sound tie-in points and repair the drainage path rather than guessing at one small spot.

When should I stop patching an older tile roof?

When the underlayment is brittle across multiple areas, leaks are recurring in unrelated locations, or each repair must tie into more failing material, a partial or full tile lift-and-lay can be better value than repeated spot repairs.

Do you offer emergency tile roof repairs during rainy season?

Yes, scheduling and safe access permitting. Emergency work may stabilize the immediate leak first, followed by a permanent repair once the roof is dry enough to open and rebuild correctly.
