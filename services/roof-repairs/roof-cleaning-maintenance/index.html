Drainage-first maintenance

We clear the places debris causes problems.

This is not a cosmetic wash service. It is practical roof maintenance focused on keeping drainage paths open and noticing problems while they are still visible.

01

Gutters & downspout entries

Remove roof debris at the gutter line and check the roof-to-gutter path so water can leave the roof efficiently.

02

Valleys, crickets & transitions

Clear leaves and dirt where roof planes meet. These collection points can hold moisture and direct water where it should not sit.

03

Flat-roof drains & scuppers

Remove debris around drains and confirm the visible drain path is open. These areas deserve attention before every storm.

04

Roof-surface debris

Remove pine needles, leaves and buildup that can collect behind shingles, around flashings, and beneath tile edges.

Why schedule it

Debris changes how water behaves on a roof.

On shingle roofs, pine needles and dirt can trap moisture and interrupt normal drainage. On tile roofs, leaves collect in valleys and at transitions. On flat roofs, blocked drains can allow water to pond. The goal is simple: remove buildup before it becomes a drainage or roof-layer problem.

We recommend checking drainage before storm season and whenever nearby trees shed heavily.

Straight answers

Roof cleaning and maintenance questions.

Should gutters and roofs be cleaned before storms?

Yes. Clearing visible debris from gutters, valleys, drains and scuppers before storm season helps water move where the roof is designed to send it.

Do you pressure wash roofs?

No. This service is drainage and debris maintenance, not pressure washing or cosmetic roof washing.

How often should I schedule maintenance?

At least before storm season, and more often for properties with heavy tree cover, pine needles, flat-roof drains, or complex valleys.

Will you find roof repairs while cleaning?

We document visible concerns such as damaged tile, lifted shingles, drainage issues or deteriorated details and explain the appropriate next step separately.
