Thin stone, substantial overlap

Slate is the visible roof. Underlayment and flashing protect the difficult areas.

Each slate is long and thin. Much of it is concealed by the courses above, including the two nails commonly used near its upper end.

1

Slate sheds the weather

Properly lapped natural slate creates multiple layers of durable stone. The amount of exposure and headlap depends on the slate size, roof slope and installation.

2

Fasteners are concealed

Slate is normally secured near the upper portion of each piece. The next courses cover those fasteners, which is why removing one slate requires a specialized tool.

3

Hidden materials still age

The stone can remain attractive while older underlayment, fasteners and flashing at valleys, walls and penetrations deteriorate beneath it.

A broken slate and a leaking slate roof are not always the same repair.

One damaged piece may need replacement. A recurring leak can require opening a larger section to reach failed underlayment or flashing and tie the repair into sound material.

The specialized tool

It is called a slate ripper

A slate ripper, also called a slater’s ripper or slate rip, is a long, thin steel bar designed to slide beneath overlapping slate. Its hooked or arrow-shaped end catches the concealed nail. The handle is struck with a hammer to pull or shear the nail.

Once both nails are released, the damaged slate can slide out. That narrow access is what makes a slate ripper more practical than trying to force a thick pry bar or short reciprocating-saw blade beneath several courses.

Step 1

Locate the damaged slate

We assess the surrounding pieces, likely leak path and safe access before disturbing the roof.

Step 2

Release both nails

The slate ripper slides beneath the piece and is worked upward until its hook catches each concealed fastener.

Step 3

Slide the slate out

After the nails are pulled or sheared, the thin slate is removed without unnecessarily lifting entire courses.

Step 4

Install the replacement

A compatible slate is sized and secured with an appropriate nail-and-bib or slate-hook repair detail.

What causes slate roof problems

The stone is durable, but every part of the assembly still matters

01

Broken, cracked or slipped slate

Foot traffic, impact, failed fasteners and natural defects can leave a piece cracked, loose or missing. Broken slate also exposes the layers below.

02

Aged underlayment

Underlayment does not last forever. When it becomes brittle across a section, replacing one visible slate may not address the full water path.

03

Failed flashing

Valleys, chimneys, walls, skylights and pipes interrupt the slate field. Corroded, undersized or poorly tied-in metal can leak even when nearby slate is intact.

04

Transitions and drainage

Changes in roof slope or direction concentrate water. Debris and improper repairs can redirect it beneath the slate or sideways across underlayment.

05

Improper walking or repairs

Natural slate is thin and can break under concentrated pressure. Access, staging and tool placement must be planned to avoid creating more damage.

06

Fastener deterioration

When nails corrode or lose their hold, otherwise serviceable slate can begin to slip. The repair must secure the new or reusable piece without damaging adjacent courses.

Slate roof repair FAQ

Questions homeowners ask about natural slate

What is the long hooked tool used to remove slate?

It is a slate ripper, also called a slater’s ripper or slate rip. It slides beneath the slate and catches the concealed nails. Striking the handle with a hammer pulls or shears the fasteners so the slate can slide out.

Can one broken slate be replaced without removing the whole section?

Yes, when the damage is isolated. The old slate can be removed with a slate ripper and a compatible replacement secured using an appropriate slate repair detail.

Why is slate roof repair expensive?

Natural slate is costly, fragile under concentrated pressure and often difficult to match. Safe access, removal of concealed fasteners and careful staging take more time than many ordinary tile repairs.

Can the original slate be reused during a sectional repair?

Often, yes. Serviceable slate can be carefully removed, sorted and reinstalled after underlayment or flashing is replaced. Some breakage and replacement material should still be anticipated.

Does underlayment matter beneath slate?

Yes. Slate sheds most weather, but underlayment provides secondary protection and is especially important while a repair area is open. Valleys, walls and penetrations also depend on properly integrated flashing.
