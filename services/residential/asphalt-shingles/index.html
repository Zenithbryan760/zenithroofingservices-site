The first decision happens underneath

We inspect the attic and substrate before we price the roof assembly.

Older Southern California homes may have plywood, OSB, solid planks, tongue-and-groove boards, skip sheathing or a former wood-shake deck. Those conditions change the tear-off, ventilation and nailing-surface requirements.

Our practical standard: skip sheathing, former shake decks and plank decks with excessive gaps receive a continuous plywood or OSB overlay. We commonly specify 7/16-inch OSB or 15/32-inch CDX, subject to the existing structure, engineering and applicable requirements.

Even sound tongue-and-groove deserves careful review because shingle nail zones must consistently land in solid material. We explain the visible conditions and the contract unit prices for concealed wood replacement before work begins.

Slope determines the assembly

We do not install shingles on roofs below 2:12.

A roof that looks like a shingle roof may include low-slope sections—or even an older tar-and-gravel roof hidden below a later overlay. We verify pitch and exposed conditions before choosing the dry-in method.

Below 2:12

Use a low-slope system

Asphalt shingles are not our solution here. We recommend a compatible flat or low-slope roof system.

2:12 to under 4:12

Enhanced underlayment

The roof may require a full self-adhered membrane or a double-underlayment assembly with 50% overlap, selected to match the manufacturer and project requirements.

4:12 and steeper

Standard system dry-in

A compatible single synthetic underlayment is commonly used with the complete manufacturer system, subject to the specified product and conditions.

Exact components and installation details follow the selected manufacturer instructions, approved scope and applicable code—not a generic one-size-fits-all recipe.

A note about old shake and skip-sheathed roofs

Opening these roofs can expose the attic directly to weather and pests. Zenith does not perform insulation work. If insulation replacement is planned, coordinate it with a qualified insulation contractor; in many cases it is safest to close and dry in the roof promptly, then complete insulation work afterward.

Common asphalt shingle questions

What property owners ask before tear-off.

Can you confirm every existing roof layer before work starts?

Not always. Mastic around penetrations, roof edges and low-slope sections may provide clues, but the exact assembly can remain hidden until tear-off. Our proposal identifies unit prices for extra layers and concealed wood work.

What happens if you find skip sheathing, wood shakes or wide plank gaps?

We establish a suitable continuous nailing surface, commonly with an approved plywood or OSB overlay. The exact material and fastening plan depend on the structure and documented scope.

Can asphalt shingles go on a roof below 2:12?

Zenith does not install asphalt shingles below 2:12. We evaluate those areas for a compatible low-slope roof system instead.

Which underlayment will you use?

That depends on slope, shingle manufacturer and conditions. Steeper roofs commonly receive a compatible synthetic underlayment. Roofs from 2:12 to under 4:12 require an enhanced assembly such as approved full-roof self-adhered protection or double underlayment with 50% overlap.

Do you install ventilation during a shingle replacement?

We evaluate intake and exhaust for the actual attic and roof. The proposal may specify fire-rated O'Hagin vents, dormers, solar attic ventilation or another compatible solution; no single vent type is correct for every property.

What happens at project completion?

We remove debris, blow off the roof, clean the gutters and work area, perform a final magnetic sweep, share project photos and provide the invoice and applicable warranty documentation.
