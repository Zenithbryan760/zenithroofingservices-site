Start with roof pitch

Slope determines which systems belong on the roof.

Final specifications must follow the adopted code, local requirements and the selected manufacturer’s listed installation instructions.

2:12 and steeper

Asphalt shingles

Asphalt shingles are permitted at 2:12 or greater. Roofs from 2:12 to under 4:12 require the code-prescribed special underlayment application. Zenith does not install shingles below 2:12.

Learn about shingle replacement
2½:12 and steeper

Clay & concrete tile

Standard clay and concrete tile begin at 2½:12. From 2½:12 to under 4:12, code requires a double-underlayment application; 4:12 and steeper uses the standard prescribed application.

Learn about tile replacement
Low-slope assemblies

Membrane roof systems

Roofs below steep-slope covering limits need a listed low-slope assembly selected for drainage, deck, use and existing conditions—not shingles or standard tile forced onto the wrong pitch.

Explore low-slope roofing

Important: Below 2½:12, standard clay or concrete tile is not used unless a specifically approved/listed assembly allows it. Existing reroofs and unusual assemblies may require project-specific review.

Roof replacement FAQ

Questions property owners ask before choosing a system.

How do I know which roofing system is best?

We start with slope, deck, drainage, existing materials and property use. Then we compare only systems that fit those conditions.

Can shingles be installed below a 2:12 slope?

No. Zenith does not install asphalt shingles below 2:12. Low-slope areas require a compatible listed membrane assembly.

Can tile be installed below 2½:12?

Standard clay and concrete tile are not used below 2½:12 unless a specifically approved/listed assembly permits it. Low pitches need project-specific review.

Do you replace residential and commercial roofs?

Yes. We work on homes, multifamily properties and commercial buildings using steep-slope and low-slope systems appropriate to each structure.

Can you combine systems on one property?

Yes. Many buildings have shingle or tile pitched areas plus low-slope patios, additions or dead valleys. Those transitions must be designed as part of the complete roof.
