Before we spec the roof

First, we find out what is already there.

Flat and low-slope roofs do not always have an accessible attic. When they do, we look from below. When access is tight or unavailable, a controlled roof core can help reveal the existing layers before removal begins.

01

Check the deck

We look for accessible signs of deck or substrate problems and make sure the roof can receive the system being considered.

Confirm existing layers

We determine whether we are sweeping off tar and gravel, removing an existing membrane, or opening a roof with several prior layers.

03

Read the details

Walls, counterflashing, drains, equipment, pipes and clearance at transitions affect the installation method and sometimes the system choice.

One material, two very different assemblies

Single-layer for limited applications. Two-ply protection where the building depends on it.

Patios & select non-livable areas

Single-layer cap system

A lighter assembly can be appropriate for certain patio covers and similar areas when the roof, substrate and use below make it a responsible fit.

  • Fiberglass base sheet
  • Metal cap fasteners
  • Target flashings at pipes and penetrations
  • Primed metals and cap sheet

Typical expectation: about 10–12 years, depending on conditions, exposure and maintenance.

Livable space & stronger protection

Two-ply modified-bitumen system

For a roof protecting the property below, we generally recommend building the full two-ply assembly rather than relying on a single cap sheet alone.

  • Fiberglass base sheet with metal cap fasteners
  • Smooth modified-bitumen ply across the roof
  • Flashing work at transitions and penetrations
  • Primed metals and a final cap sheet

Built for a longer service life—often 20+ years when the roof and installation conditions support it.

Service-life ranges are general guidance, not a promise or warranty. The actual roof assembly, drainage, exposure, maintenance and site conditions matter.

How Zenith builds it

A clean sequence protects the work that comes next.

Each roof has its own details, but this is the field process we follow when a full modified-bitumen installation is the right answer.

  1. Remove or prepare the existing roof.We sweep tar and gravel where required, remove the existing roof when specified, and clear loose material so the substrate can be properly prepared.
  2. Install the fiberglass base.The base sheet is installed and secured with metal cap fasteners. Loose sand is blown off before the next layer goes down.
  3. Build the chosen assembly.For a two-ply roof, the smooth layer runs across the roof deck before the cap. A single-layer roof follows its appropriate base, detail and cap sequence.
  4. Handle flashings and metals before the cap.Pipes, penetrations, edges and flashings are detailed. Metals are primed so the membrane bonds correctly at those transitions.
  5. Install the cap sheet and close out.The cap sheet completes the waterproofing layer. We clean the site and complete the final work around the roof details.

The things that decide whether it makes sense

Not every flat roof is a good torch-down candidate.

Torch-down is a proven modified-bitumen method, but the surrounding property conditions matter just as much as the field membrane.

Vegetation & fire exposure

On roofs close to heavy trees or where site fire risk is a concern, we evaluate whether another compatible system is the better fit.

Tight counterflashing

If counterflashing sits too close to the roof, it may need to be removed and rebuilt to create a proper transition—or the system choice may need to change.

Equipment-dense roofs

HVAC, skylights, conduit and service paths create penetrations and transition work that must be planned before membrane installation.

Straight answers

Torch-down roofing FAQ

The roof system should match the building and its details. Here is how we approach the most common questions.

Do you always recommend two-ply torch-down?

Not always. A single cap system can make sense for a limited application such as a patio cover. For a roof over livable space or protecting the building below, we generally recommend the more robust two-ply approach.

What is included in a two-ply modified-bitumen roof?

The assembly typically begins with a fiberglass base sheet mechanically fastened with metal caps, then a smooth modified-bitumen ply, flashing work, primed metals and the final cap sheet. Exact details depend on the roof.

Why do metals need primer before the cap sheet?

Primer helps the membrane properly adhere at metal edges, flashings and other transitions. Those details are common places for roof failures when they are not handled correctly.

Can you install torch-down around counterflashing?

Sometimes. Where existing counterflashing is too close to the roof surface, it may need to be removed and replaced or the roof may require another compatible approach that works better with the transition.

Can a roof near trees or high fire exposure use torch-down?

We evaluate the full site conditions. In some settings, another compatible roofing method may be the more responsible recommendation.
