Attachment options

Two common ways to build a TPO roof.

Both approaches can perform well when the deck, fastener pattern, adhesive, insulation and membrane are part of a compatible system.

01

Mechanically attached

Approved insulation and membrane components are secured to a suitable deck with plates and fasteners. The fastening pattern is selected for the assembly and roof zones.

  • Useful where the deck can accept the specified fasteners
  • Can use polyiso, cover board or an approved recover board
  • Requires deliberate perimeter, corner and penetration detailing
02

Fully adhered

Compatible insulation and membrane layers are bonded with an approved adhesive system. This can be appropriate where visible fasteners below or substrate conditions make mechanical attachment undesirable.

  • Creates a smooth, continuously bonded membrane field
  • Adhesive selection follows substrate and manufacturer requirements
  • Walls and vertical surfaces are commonly fully adhered
Other engineered attachment methods exist. Zenith scopes the project around tested components and the requirements of the selected manufacturer—not a one-size-fits-all detail.

Recover and overlay options

An overlay is an engineered decision—not a shortcut.

Some existing roofs may qualify for an approved recover assembly using fan-fold, insulation or a cover board. Others need removal so wet materials, damaged decking or incompatible layers can be corrected.

  • Confirm the existing assembly and deck can accept the new system
  • Check for moisture and damaged substrate before covering it
  • Confirm recover eligibility with the selected manufacturer
  • Coordinate edge heights, drains, curbs and transitions after added thickness

Heat-welded seam control

The sheets become a roof at the welds.

Field seams are welded with calibrated hot-air equipment. Corners, curbs, pipes and transitions receive compatible hand-welded detail work.

Membrane surfaces are prepared as required, automatic welding equipment is adjusted for site conditions, and the crew hand-rolls and checks details. This is where clean preparation and consistent workmanship matter most.

TPO roofing questions

What property owners ask before choosing a system.

The answers depend on the roof in front of us. A site evaluation turns these general options into a buildable scope.

What is the difference between mechanically attached and fully adhered TPO?+

Mechanically attached systems use approved plates and fasteners into a suitable deck. Fully adhered systems bond compatible assembly components with approved adhesive. Deck, wind design, building use and manufacturer requirements guide the choice.

Can TPO be installed over an existing roof?+

Sometimes. The current roof must qualify for an approved recover assembly. Existing moisture, deck damage, incompatible materials, edge heights or drainage conditions may require removal instead.

Can TPO be used on a residential low-slope roof?+

Yes, when the substrate, slope, details, permit requirements and selected manufacturer assembly support TPO. Residential and manufactured-home roofs are evaluated individually.

How are TPO seams sealed?+

TPO seams are joined with calibrated hot-air welding equipment. Field seams can be completed with an automatic welder, while corners and roof details are hand-welded and checked.

Does Zenith use one standard membrane thickness or manufacturer?+

No. We select the membrane specification, insulation and attachment method around the roof, building requirements and an approved compatible system. The process is designed for the property rather than limited to one manufacturer or one membrane thickness.
