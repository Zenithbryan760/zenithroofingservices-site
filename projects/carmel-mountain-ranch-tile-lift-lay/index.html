The project decision

The tile still had value. The system beneath it needed to be rebuilt.

The two-story home had serviceable Monier Homestead concrete tiles, but the aging underlayment and hidden roof details were no longer a reliable foundation for another isolated repair. A lift and lay allowed the original tile to be preserved while the assembly below it was opened, inspected and corrected.

Once the tile and old underlayment were removed, localized damaged wood was replaced. Zenith then installed the new underlayment and flashing system before resetting the existing tile across the completed roof sections.

What Zenith completed

Opened, repaired, layered and reset.

This was a complete tile lift-and-relay scope, not a surface coating or another patch over aging underlayment.

The finished result

The original tile returned over a renewed roof system.

From the exterior, the home retained the established Monier Homestead tile profile. The major improvement is below the surface: repaired decking, two layers of Titanium UDL50, self-adhered PSU30 at selected details, and renewed flashings documented before the roof was closed.

  • Existing concrete tile reused where serviceable
  • Localized damaged wood replaced
  • New valley metal and drip-edge flashing
  • Upgraded pipe flashing
Homeowner lesson

A tile roof can look serviceable while the underlayment beneath it is aging.

Concrete tile can often outlast the original waterproofing below it. When the tile remains reusable, a properly scoped lift and lay can renew the deck, underlayment and flashing system without requiring a completely new tile roof.
